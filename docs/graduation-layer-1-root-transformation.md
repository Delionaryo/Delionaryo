# DELIONARYO Graduation System
## Layer 1 — Root Transformation: Survival → Stability Foundation

Status: Framework v1.0 — professional foundation before production scoring calibration.

### Purpose
Layer 1 is designed for members whose financial condition is primarily in survival mode. It does not label poverty as a character defect. It diagnoses constraints across personal, economic, environmental, behavioral, and system dimensions, then matches each constraint with a constructive wealth-creation capability.

### Graduation Principle
Completion is not based on reading content. A member progresses through:

Baseline → Diagnosis → Prescribed Learning → Action → Evidence → Reassessment → Graduation Review

A member does not need to be wealthy to graduate Layer 1. The objective is to demonstrate movement from unmanaged survival toward a measurable stability foundation.

## The 6×6 Root Model

### Poverty Root 1 — Resource Insufficiency & Income Fragility
Definition: Available income/resources are insufficient, irregular, highly concentrated, or too fragile to reliably protect essential needs.
Indicators: essential-cost coverage, income reliability, number/concentration of income sources, frequency of cash shortages, dependence on emergency borrowing/support.
Corresponding Wealth Root: **Earning Capacity & Value Creation**
Development objective: strengthen useful skills, employability/enterprise capacity, market value, and reliable legitimate earning ability.

### Poverty Root 2 — Financial Capability & Information Gap
Definition: Limited practical knowledge or access to reliable information reduces the quality of financial decisions.
Indicators: ability to calculate cash flow, debt cost, margin, break-even, distinguish assets/liabilities/productive resources, evaluate financial claims and scams.
Corresponding Wealth Root: **Financial Intelligence & Decision Quality**
Development objective: understand money flow, costs, risk, trade-offs, business economics, and evidence-based decisions.

### Poverty Root 3 — Resource Leakage & Weak Control Systems
Definition: Resources enter but are not consistently allocated, protected, tracked, or directed toward priorities.
Indicators: untracked spending, repeated penalties/leaks, absence of allocation rules, recurring depletion before payday, lack of productive-resource discipline.
Corresponding Wealth Root: **Resource Control & Productive Allocation**
Development objective: establish money-flow visibility, priority allocation, DPBS discipline where feasible, protection boundaries, and controlled productive resources.

### Poverty Root 4 — Capability-to-Market & Opportunity Constraint
Definition: A person may have effort or skills but lacks effective access, positioning, tools, networks, customers, employment pathways, capital access, digital access, location advantages, or channels through which value can be exchanged.
Indicators: market access, job/customer pipeline, usable tools/connectivity, opportunity awareness, ability to present an offer, geographic/social constraints.
Corresponding Wealth Root: **Opportunity Access & Enterprise Execution**
Development objective: connect useful capability to legitimate opportunities through employment improvement, services, products, marketplace activity, partnerships, and appropriate tools.

### Poverty Root 5 — Behavioral, Psychological & Execution Friction
Definition: Scarcity pressure, learned beliefs, short-term bias, avoidance, low self-efficacy, inconsistency, or weak execution routines interfere with otherwise possible improvements. This root must never be used to blame people for structural constraints.
Indicators: plan-follow-through, impulse/avoidance patterns, repeated abandonment, financial trigger responses, consistency of weekly actions, willingness to review evidence and correct behavior.
Corresponding Wealth Root: **Agency, Discipline & Adaptive Execution**
Development objective: build realistic routines, decision rules, self-leadership, experimentation, correction, and consistent execution under constraints.

### Poverty Root 6 — Protection, Social-System & Stewardship Fragility
Definition: Progress remains vulnerable because of shocks, harmful obligations, weak support structures, poor protection, governance gaps, exploitative environments, or lack of long-term purpose and responsibility systems.
Indicators: emergency vulnerability, health/family shocks, fraud exposure, documentation/records, household alignment, support network quality, risk controls, purpose and responsibility clarity.
Corresponding Wealth Root: **Resilience, Stewardship & Legacy Capacity**
Development objective: protect progress, strengthen trustworthy systems and relationships, manage risk, exercise integrity, align resources with responsibility/purpose, and eventually transfer capability.

## Assessment Design
Each poverty root and wealth root will use multiple observable indicators rather than one subjective question. Initial scoring can use a 0–4 response scale per indicator:

0 = severe constraint / no system
1 = high constraint / rarely demonstrated
2 = developing / inconsistent
3 = functional / usually demonstrated
4 = strong / consistently demonstrated with evidence

Scores must be interpreted as diagnostic signals, not clinical, moral, credit, or investment-risk ratings.

### Root Priority Logic
Priority is determined by a combination of:
- severity of constraint;
- effect on essential stability;
- urgency/risk;
- dependency (whether solving it unlocks other roots);
- feasibility of near-term intervention.

The lowest numerical score is therefore not automatically the first intervention.

## Layer 1 Evidence Requirements
Evidence should be proportionate to the member's circumstances. Examples include:
- completed 30-day money-flow baseline;
- verified essential-cost number;
- current obligation/debt map where applicable;
- weekly review records;
- documented skill/capability development;
- evidence of employment, offer, customer, marketplace, or other value-creation activity;
- sustainable productive-resource allocation where feasible;
- reduced recurring leak or financial pressure point;
- protection/risk action;
- before/after root reassessment.

No member should be failed merely because a fixed savings percentage is impossible while essential needs are unmet.

## Proposed Layer 1 Graduation Gate
Layer 1 graduation requires all of the following, subject to later calibration:
1. Baseline assessment completed honestly.
2. Primary and secondary constraints identified.
3. Prescribed learning/actions completed for priority roots.
4. Minimum evidence period completed (recommended initial design: 30–90 days depending on intervention).
5. Essential money flow is visible and reviewed regularly.
6. At least one demonstrated improvement in earning capacity, resource control, opportunity execution, or resilience.
7. No critical unresolved root is falsely marked complete; critical structural constraints may instead require a documented mitigation/referral plan.
8. Reassessment demonstrates improved system capability, not merely quiz knowledge.
9. Member creates the next-stage Stability Plan.

### Graduation Output
Upon passing, record:
- baseline root profile;
- final root profile;
- strongest improvement;
- remaining constraints;
- evidence references;
- Stability Plan;
- graduation date;
- next prescribed DELIONARYO layer.

## DELIONARYO Routing Map
Root 1 → earning-capacity learning, skills/value creation, income-generation pathways.
Root 2 → financial literacy, Money Flow, Business Calculator, relevant Campus learning.
Root 3 → Sovereign Budget & Flow, DPBS principles, trackers and control systems.
Root 4 → Marketplace, product/service execution, opportunity and customer-access training.
Root 5 → Money's Transformation, Money Belief Reset, Poverty Story Rewrite, Weekly Freedom Tracker.
Root 6 → protection, Purpose Alignment Map, Wealth Legacy Plan, stewardship and resilience learning.

## Professional Safeguards
- Do not claim the assessment proves the cause of a person's poverty.
- Do not reduce structural poverty to mindset.
- Do not promise wealth, income, financial sovereignty, or graduation outcomes.
- Do not use this educational score as a credit score, lending decision, employment screening, insurance rating, or investment suitability test.
- Keep financial, mental-health, legal, debt, and social-service referrals outside the system's competence when professional intervention is needed.
- Calibrate thresholds using real member data before presenting scores as validated measures.

## Product Architecture
Layer 1 should ultimately support:
Assessment → Root Profile → Priority Root → Wealth Root Prescription → Campus Content → Tool → Action Plan → Evidence Locker → Weekly Check-in → Reassessment → Graduation Review.

This document is the source-of-truth foundation for the first DELIONARYO graduation layer. UI implementation and scoring thresholds should follow this model rather than inventing independent definitions in each app.
